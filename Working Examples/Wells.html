General information

Eligibility for the Canada Pension Plan Disability Benefits

The CPP disability benefit and the CPP post-retirement disability benefit are taxable monthly payments available to people who made contributions to CPP, are under the age 65 and are not able to work regularly at any job because of a disability.

The CPP disability benefit is intended for individuals who are not receiving a CPP retirement pension, and the CPP post-retirement disability benefit is intended for individuals who become disabled after they begin to receive a CPP retirement pension.

A benefit may also be available to their children.

How do I qualify for CPP disability benefits?

To qualify for CPP disability benefits, 3 conditions must be met:

  1. You must be under the age of 65

  2. You must have the minimum amount of valid CPP contributions

    Contributions to the CPP are made while you are working.

    Service Canada will review your contribution history to determine if you made the minimum amount of valid CPP contributions to qualify for CPP disability benefits.

    The disability benefit amount you could qualify for is based on how long and much you contributed to the CPP plus a flat rate. The post-retirement disability benefit is a flat rate component of the CPP disability benefit.

    The information you provide in Information about you and certain provisions along with the information on your account will help us determine if you made the minimum amount of valid contributions to the CPP.

  3. You must have a mental or physical medical condition(s) that prevents you from regularly working at any job. The medical condition(s) must be found to be both severe and prolonged when you last met the minimum amount of valid CPP contributions to qualify, and you must have been unable to work continuously since then.

    For the CPP:

    Severe means that you have a mental and/or physical disability that regularly stops you from doing any type of substantially gainful work.

    Substantially gainful work or occupation is considered to be any profession or work one might pursue to earn a living. If the total amount of earnings from this work is more than 12 times the maximum monthly CPP disability benefit amount, a person is normally considered to be doing substantially gainful work.

    Prolonged means that the disability is long-term and of indefinite duration or is likely to result in death.

    Service Canada will review the information you provide along with the medical information we receive from your doctor, nurse practitioner, insurance company or provincial/territorial agency. This will help us determine how your medical condition(s) impacts your capability to perform work-related activities.

If you need time to complete the application

Past applicants have reported that it takes time to complete the application. Some have suggested that it was easier to complete it in multiple sittings. You may want to consider saving your application after each section.

You must keep in mind that the date Service Canada receives your application is important as it could affect when your benefits start.

If you have been diagnosed with a terminal illness

For the purposes of CPP, a terminal medical condition is a disease or illness that cannot be cured or adequately treated and is reasonably expected to result in the death in 6 months.

Applications from patients with a terminal illness receive priority handling. Our goal is to make a decision within 5 business days of receiving your complete Terminal Illness application.

Medical report

If you are currently receiving a disability benefit from an insurance company or a provincial/territorial agency:

  • You can ask them to send us your most current medical report(s).

If you are not currently receiving a disability benefit from an insurance company or a provincial/territorial agency:

  • Complete Sections 1 and 2 of the Medical Report for Canada Pension Plan Disability Benefits (ISP2519)
  • Write/type your Social Insurance Number at the top of each page.
  • Sign all areas that require your signature
  • Ask your doctor or nurse practitioner to complete Section 3 to 9 and ask them to mail it to the nearest Service Canada office.

DO NO WAIT for your doctor or nurse practitioner to complete the Medical report before transmitting your complete application to Service Canada. The date Service Canada receives your application could affect when your benefit starts.

Service Canada will help you pay for the cost of the Medical report by paying up to $85.00 directly to your doctor or nurse practitioner. Any money owing over this amount is your responsibility.

If you are eligible for more than one CPP benefit

If you are eligible for both a CPP survivor's benefit and a disability pension, they will be combined into a single monthly payment. However, the combined amount you receive cannot be greater than the maximum CPP disability pension, which is more than the maximum CPP survivor's benefit.

Post-Retirement Disability Benefit

The Post-Retirement Disability Benefit is intended for Canada Pension Plan retirement pension beneficiaries found to be disabled but not eligible for a disability pension due to being CPP retirement pension beneficiaries for more than 15 months. Applicants who have made sufficient contributions may be eligible for this benefit in addition to their retirement pension.

The Post-Retirement Disability Benefit is paid in addition to the CPP retirement pension you are receiving, until the age of 65.

Benefits for children under age 25

The Canada Pension Plan (CPP) children's benefits provide monthly payments to the dependent children of a disabled CPP contributors.

The children must be either:

  • Under age 18; or
  • Under age 25 and in attendance at a recognized school or university

A maximum of two children's benefit can be paid to a child.

Eligibility

To be eligible, the child must be:

  • The natural child of the contributor
  • A child adopted legally or in fact by the contributor while under the age of 21

A child may be eligible if the parent or guardian:

  • Is receiving a CPP disability benefit (either the disability or the post-retirement disability benefit)

When to apply

Dependent children, or their parent or guardian, should complete an application when any of the following happens:

  • a parent or guardian has applied for a disability benefit (you do not have to wait for the benefit to be approved before applying);
  • when a child comes into the decision-making responsibility and parenting time of a parent or guardian who receives a disability benefit;
  • a parent or guardian dies.

You should apply as soon as possible. If you delay, you might lose benefits. The Canada Pension Plan can only make back payments for up to 12 months.

Note: Effective January 1, 2025, if you, or anyone else, were receiving Disabled Contributor’s Children’s Benefits, those benefits may continue after the contributor reaches age 65.

Recover application in progress

The disability benefit application is long and you may not be able to complete it in one session. You can save your partially completed application at any time and return to it within 90 days. Service Canada will not process your application until it is complete and you have submitted it. If you have an application in progress and you decide to start a new application, your application in progress will be deleted.

Apply for My CPP Disability Benefits

You must indicate if you have been informed by your physician or nurse practitioner if your medical condition is considered a terminal illness.

If your medical condition is not considered a terminal illness you must indicate if you have been diagnosed with one of the 41 medical conditions.

Information about you

Change of address

People living in Canada may view and change their address, telephone number and payment destination online.

Exceptions:

  • If you have an address outside Canada, you can only view your information online. It is not yet possible to change foreign addresses or direct deposit online. Sorry for the inconvenience. Please contact us to update your information. We are working to soon offer the possibility to update your personal information online.
  • If you receive children's benefits and are between the ages of 18 and 25, you may not be able to access this online service due to system limitations. If you wish to make changes, please contact us.
  • If you receive a Quebec Pension Plan benefit, please contact Retraite Québec for more information.

Postal code format

Please enter the postal code of your new address. Postal codes are six characters long and are always in letter-number format, such as: A1A 1A1, (where A = any letter and 1 = any number). If you have trouble, check that the letters "O" or lowercase "L" are not substituted for zeros or ones, or vice-versa. If you do not know your postal code you can look it up on the Canada Post Web site. At this time, postal codes can only be updated online for Canadian addresses. Please contact us for changes to addresses outside Canada.

Mandatory fields

Fields marked with an asterisk (*) must be completed. Some fields may be filled in based on the postal code you entered. To change the information, select either "Enter a different postal code" or "Enter a different address for this postal code".

Number suffix

Some addresses include a suffix, for example: 123 ½ Main St., where ½ is the suffix and not an apartment number. If you do not have a number suffix, please leave this field blank.

Delivery mode

If you have mail delivery to your door, please select "None" from the pull-down menu.

Otherwise, select from the following methods of mail delivery:

  • MR - mobile route is a variation on door-to-door delivery, when the street is served by a delivery route service.
  • PO Box - post office box
  • RR - rural route is a delivery route service from a delivery installation.
  • GD - general delivery is mail held at a post office until it is picked up in person.
  • SS - suburban service is mail delivered to group mail boxes; usually found near, or on the edge of, urban areas.

Address length

The database where address information is stored allows for a maximum of 30 characters per line, including spaces and punctuation. If longer, your address must be shortened. Note: Street types such as Blvd (Boulevard) and Ave (Avenue) are part of your address and should not be removed.

Entering a future effective date

When you select "Change" to enter your new information, you can either accept today's date as shown, or enter an effective date up to 180 days in the future. When choosing a future effective date, you must ensure the date you are choosing is one month greater than the month you are currently in.

For example, if you want your January payment to be sent to a new destination, you must have an effective date of no later then December 31.

Direct deposit

Direct deposit is the fastest and most convenient, secure and reliable way of receiving your CPP benefits entitlement. We highly encourage you to choose direct deposit as your preferred payment option.

Should you wish to receive paper cheques on a monthly basis, please cancel your online application, then complete and submit the paper application for the CPP Disability benefit to the Department.

If your application is approved, your monthly payments will be deposited into your account at your financial institution. The account must be in your name. A joint account is also acceptable.

In order to enroll for direct deposit banking you must provide the branch financial institution and account numbers that can be found in a bank book on a bank statement or at the bottom of your cheque.

Cheque number: If you use personalized cheques, the first set of numbers is your three-digit personal cheque number. Do not enter this number.

Branch number: The second set of numbers on personalized cheques, or the first set of numbers on non-personalized cheques, is your five-digit branch number. Enter this number in the first entry field.

Institution number: The next set of numbers is your three-digit institution number. Enter this number in the second entry field.

Account number: The last set of numbers is your bank account number. The number of digits varies depending on the financial institution. Enter your account number in the third entry field.

Telephone numbers

Where you select to add a telephone number, you have the option to include a home, mobile and work number.

A telephone number is mandatory when applying for the CPP disability benefit.

Please enter your new telephone number as three-digit area code, followed by a seven-digit phone number (e.g. 123 456-7890).

Marital status

Marital status is any of several distinct options that describe a person's relationship with a partner. Married, single, divorced, common-law, living apart for reasons beyond your control and widowed are examples of marital status.

Previous relationship

The CPP contributions you and your spouse or common-law partner made during the time you lived together can be equally divided after a divorce or separation. This is called credit splitting.

Credits can be divided even if one spouse or common-law partner did not make contributions to the CPP. Credit splitting may help you qualify for benefits and can affect the amount of any current or future benefits under the CPP program for both you and your former spouse or common-law partner.

Information about your children

Child rearing provision

The child rearing provision may help you qualify for or receive a higher Canada Pension Plan benefit amount. The amount of benefits paid under the Canada Pension Plan is based on how long and how much you contributed to the Plan while you were working, and in some cases, your age when your benefit begins. Periods of time when you had no or low earnings normally result in a lower benefit amount. If you were not working or had low earnings while caring for a child under the age of seven, the child rearing provision can be used to exclude these periods of time form the calculation of your benefit. This may help you qualify for benefits or increase the benefit amount you can receive.

Functional assessment

We asked you to state the medical condition(s) that prevents you from working regularly. With these questions, we would like you to tell us how the medical condition(s) affects your ability to work. The answers and additional information you provide will be considered along with the medical information provided by your doctor, nurse practitioner, insurance company or provincial/territorial agency.

As you are answering the questions, think about all of your physical and mental limitations, regardless of what medical problem is causing them. Focus on what you can do, not how you feel.

Next, think about what it means to be a worker. All jobs area different, but working means you must be able to:

  • Get hired or create your own job;
  • Get ready for work;
  • Travel to and from work;
  • Deal with co-workers and clients;
  • Deliver a quality product or service; and
  • Follow a work schedule set by your employer and/or client

Then compare your limitations to the demands of work, and provide your rating on the next few pages based on your ability level most days. Assume you are using your aids such as crutches, cane, artificial limb, splints, braces, wheelchairs, hearing aid, service animal or adaptive computer equipment.

If you do not have any limitations with the ability being assessed, you can check the box at the top of each question block.

If you have any additional information about your abilities, you can provide the details in the area following each question block. The following example could help you with the explanation/information you may want to provide in these areas.

Examples

Physical abilities
It is very difficult for me to remain standing for more than 10 minutes at any given time because of my back pain, even on my good days (one or two days a week). Up until a year ago, I was able to do this without a problem. I am most comfortable lying down. Hot baths help, but only briefly.
Behaviours and emotional abilities
In the last few months, my depression has got worse. I have a hard time getting myself out of bed most mornings (four to six days a week) because I feel so down. I find myself crying for no reason and I am often irritable with others. On my good days, I spend some time with other people, but on my bad days, I cannot get myself to leave my home. I stopped volunteering for my son's hockey league because it's too hard being around others.
Communication and thinking abilities
In the last year, my fibromyalgia has made it very hard to sleep at night. On my good days (one or two days a week), I am able to sleep up to four hours, but on my bad days, I cannot sleep at all. Medications for pain and sleep leave me drowsy and "spacey" the next day. Because of this, I have a lot of difficulty organizing my thoughts and finding my words when I talk to others. I cannot concentrate on what I am supposed to do most of the time. I used to read novels for pleasure, but now I can't focus my attention for more than couple of pages.
Other daily abilities
Starting last year, my fatigue has been overwhelming. I used to constantly be "on the go" running my home-based bookkeeping business, seeing new clients and driving my kids to sports and other activities. That ended when my condition flared and I had to let go all my clients because I couldn't keep up. Now, I am unable to finish doing household chores without having to sit or lie down every half hour. Even washing myself has become too hard. I cannot hold my arms up long enough to finish washing my hair because I get so tired.

Consent to communicate to communicate information to an authorized person

Personal information about you and the benefits you receive is confidential. Service Canada must have your consent to communicate with another person about your benefits.

If you receive Canada Pension Plan (CPP) benefits, you may want to consent to:

  • allowing someone you know to communicate with Service Canada for you; and
  • allowing Service Canada to give this person information about your income, your CPP payments, and your CPP contributions.

Your consent allows the person to contact Service Canada to report your change of address, but not the mailing address for your cheque. Also, the person is NOT allowed to:

  • apply for benefits for you;
  • change the bank account where your cheque is deposited; or
  • request or change federal income tax deductions from your benefits.

If Service Canada records show that a legal representative such as a trustee or someone with power of attorney is authorized to act for you, we will communicate only with that legal representative.
