General information

There are two types of Canada Pension Plan (CPP) survivor benefits: the survivor's pension and the surviving child's benefit. For the surviving spouse/common-law partner/children to be eligible for benefits, the deceased person (the contributor) must have made enough contributions to the CPP.

The Canada Pension Plan (CPP) survivor's pension is paid to the person who is the legal spouse or common-law partner of the deceased contributor at the time of their death. If you are a separated legal spouse or common-law partner, and the deceased was not living with another common-law partner when they died, you may qualify for this benefit.

The Canada Pension Plan (CPP) surviving child's benefit provides monthly payments to the dependent children of deceased CPP contributors. Children must be either under age 18 or under age 25 and in full-time or part-time attendance at a recognized school or university. If the child is under 18, this benefit is paid on their behalf to the person or agency who has decision-making responsibility and care of the child. If the child is aged 18 to 25, the benefit is paid directly to the child.

Eligibility

To qualify for the CPP survivor's pension:

  • You must have been legally married or in a common-law union with your deceased spouse or common-law partner at the time of their death; AND
  • Your deceased spouse or common-law partner must have made enough contributions to the Canada Pension Plan

The international social security agreements that Canada has with other countries may be used to satisfy these requirements.

Note: An individual who is approved for a credit split with their separated spouse or common-law partner in January 2025 or later is not eligible for a Canada Pension Plan Survivor's benefit due to the death of the same separated spouse.

How much could you receive?

The amount you receive as a surviving spouse or common-law partner will depend on:

  • whether you are younger or older than age 65
  • how much, and for how long, the deceased contributor has paid into the CPP

We first calculate the amount that the CPP retirement pension is, or would have been if the deceased had been age 65 at the time of death. Then, a further calculation is done based on the survivor's age at the time of the contributor's death.

If the survivor is:

age 65 or more then the survivor's pension is 60% of the contributor's retirement pension if the surviving spouse or common-law partner is not receiving other CPP benefits
under age 65 then the survivor's pension is 60% of the contributor's retirement pension if the surviving spouse or common-law partner is not receiving other CPP benefits plus 37.5% of the contributor's retirement pension, if the surviving spouse or common-law partner is not receiving other CPP benefits

What documents can be used as proof of death?

NOTE: Proof is not required at the time of your application.

The Canada Pension Plan program reserves the right to request supporting documents. When documents are requested, copies are acceptable; however, Service Canada may ask for an original or certified copy at any time.

The following documents will be accepted as the proof of the date of death under the CPP Act and the OAS Act:

  • Official Death Certificate issued under the authority of some level of government (domestic or foreign) where the death occurred
  • A document issued by a level of domestic or foreign government (federal, provincial/state/territorial, municipal, etc,) indicating the date of death (for example, Japanese Family Register, Portuguese Cédula Pessoal, etc.)
  • Funeral Home Burial or Death Certificate issued
    • in accordance with the custom of any religious denomination by an ordained religious leader, or
    • by the funeral director, or
    • by any person who is authorized to issue such documents through the funeral home (these may differ from province to province)
  • Medical Certificate of Death issued by the attending doctor or coroner
  • Statement by a doctor last in attendance, a coroner or a funeral director using stationery with the appropriate identifying letterhead
  • Registration of death under a provincial or territorial authority
  • Certification of death from another country, if an international agreement on social security exists with that country
  • Memorandum of Notification of Death issued by the Chief of National Defence Staff, Department of National Defence Canada, where the death of a member of the Canadian Forces occurs outside of Canada
  • Statement of Verification of Death from the Department of Veterans Affairs Canada written on the letterhead of the Department
  • Official Notification from the Administrator of the Estate appointed by a court
  • Copy of the Letters of Probate
  • Life or Group Insurance Claim provided it includes a statement signed by a medical doctor
  • An official notification written on the letterhead of a Provincial Public Trustee or Administrator of Estates

Definition of spouse and common-law partner

Under the Canada Pension Plan:

  • a spouse is a person to whom you are legally married
  • a common-law partner is a person of the opposite sex or same sex who has been living in a conjugal relationship for at least one year

Acceptable proof of legal marriage or common-law union

NOTE: Proof is not required at the time of your application.

The Canada Pension Plan program reserves the right to request supporting documents. When documents are requested, copies are acceptable; however, Service Canada may ask for an original or certified copy at any time.

Legal marriage

  • Marriage Certificate - official copy or extract of the church, synagogue, mosque, temple etc., or civil record of marriage as issued by a competent authority, or certified true copy of the record.
  • Marriage Registration Forms, if they are an official copy or extract of the record from Vital Statistics and indicate evidence of registration i.e. registration number.
  • Statutory Declaration of Legal Marriage (ISP1809B) - must contain the name of the couple, the date and the location of the marriage

Common-law union

  • Statutory Declaration of Common-law Union form – single signature (ISP3104CPP)
  • Adult Interdependent Partner Agreement (AB)
  • Certificate of Civil Union (QC)
  • Certificate of Common-Law Relationship (MB)
  • Child custody or school records
  • Cohabitation or Prenuptial Agreement
  • Domestic Partners Certificate (NS) (SK)
  • Income Tax and Benefit Returns
  • Joint: will, acct, bill, receipt, ins, contract
  • Loan Agreement with both names
  • Medical Insurance Coverage
  • Prescription/medical records indicating the nature of relationship
  • Provincial Registration Forms
  • RRSPs naming each other as beneficiaries
  • Records showing same address e.g. driver's license
  • Statements from Third Parties
  • Other type of record which reflects the nature of the relationship

Copies of original documents

Please send copies rather than original documents whenever submitting documents to Service Canada. If you must send your original documents, we suggest you send them by registered mail. We will return the original documents to you.

  • We can only accept a copy of an original document if it is readable.
  • If the document has information on more than one page, copy all pages.

Please write your Social Insurance Number on any document or copy that you send to Service Canada.

Note: If your copy is missing any of the above elements, it will not be accepted and you will have to submit a new copy. This could result in delays in processing your application. Service Canada may request an original or certified copy at any time.

Voluntary income tax deduction

Your Canada Pension Plan benefit is taxable income. If you want Service Canada to deduct income tax each month, you must indicate the amount or a percentage.

Uploading supporting documents for your application

What types of documents can I upload?

You can upload and send Service Canada supporting documents related to:

  • CPP disability benefit and post-retirement disability benefit
  • request for reconsideration for a CPP disability benefit
  • CPP death benefit
  • CPP survivor's pension and surviving child's benefit

The supporting documents can be for:

  • an application you completed online in the My Service Canada Account; or
  • a paper application that you sent Service Canada.

How to submit a document

Before uploading any document(s), you must agree to the Privacy Notice and Terms of use.

Only one document at a time can be uploaded. Maximum document size is 4 MB. Documents must be in one of the following file formats: .bmp, .gif, .jpg/.jpeg, .pdf, .png or .txt.

Virus scan

All documents will be scanned for viruses. Files that contain viruses will be securely deleted. You will see a message on the screen saying that your document contained viruses. If you make 3 attempts to upload a document that contains viruses, you will be blocked from accessing the document upload service for 24 hours.

Contact Service Canada

If you have any questions or concerns about the document(s) you are trying to submit or that you have submitted, please contact us.
