When do you want your pension to start?

Your retirement pension can start as early as the month after you turn 60 or as late as the month after you turn 70.

You must apply at least one month before you want to start receiving your retirement benefit. Then it would start the month after the date you indicate on your application, as long we receive your application on time.

Reduced pension: from age 60 to age 65

If you choose to start receiving retirement benefits before you turn 65, your pension is reduced by a set percentage for the rest of your life. The percentage is based on the number of months before age 65 that you start your pension.

The reduction percentage is based on the year you start your pension and stays the same for your entire pension.

Your pension may increase each year based on changes in the Consumer Price Index.

Full pension: at age 65

If you start receiving your pension at age 65, you will get the full pension amount you are entitled to, based on your earnings and contributions. Your pension will start the month after your 65th birthday.

Increased pension: after the age of 65

You can delay your retirement pension up to age 70. If you choose to delay it, your benefit is increased by a set percentage. The percentage is based on the number of months after age 65 that you delay receiving it. There is no financial benefit to delaying your pension beyond age 70.

Deciding when to start your retirement pension

You decide when to start your CPP retirement benefit. Your decision depends on your situation. You should think about:

  • how much and how long you have paid into the CPP
  • any other retirement income
  • if you have a company pension plan (check with your employer to see if it will be affected by your CPP pension)
  • whether or not you will work after the age of 60
  • your current health and family health history; and
  • your retirement plans

For information on Canada’s public pensions and for help estimating your personal retirement income needs, you can use the Canadian Retirement Income Calculator.

Post-Retirement Benefit

You may be eligible for a Post-Retirement Benefit if you are 60 to 70 years of age and you are working or return to work in Canada (outside Quebec) while receiving a retirement pension from the Canada Pension Plan or Quebec Pension Plan.

The Post-Retirement Benefit will allow you to increase your retirement income even if you are already receiving the maximum Canada Pension Plan pension amount. It is a secure monthly benefit that will rise with increases in the cost of living and be payable for the rest of your life.

You do not need to apply for the Post-Retirement Benefit. If you are eligible, it will be paid to you automatically.

For each year that you make a valid contribution to the Canada Pension Plan while receiving your retirement pension, you become eligible for a new Post-Retirement Benefit the following January. Like the Canada Pension Plan retirement pension, the amount of each Post-Retirement Benefit will depend on your level of earnings, the amount of Canada Pension Plan contributions you made during the previous year, and your age as of the effective date of the Post-Retirement Benefit.

Each new Post-Retirement Benefit will be added to any previously earned Post-Retirement Benefits and to any other Canada Pension Plan benefits to which you are entitled.

If you work in the same year that your retirement pension begins, we determine how much of that year’s contributions goes toward the retirement pension and how much, if any, goes toward the post-retirement benefit.

If you are under age 65, are working and receive a CPP retirement pension, you and your employer must contribute to the post-retirement benefit. If you are self-employed, you will have to contribute both the employee and employer portions.

If you are 65 years of age or older, you can choose not to contribute. For more information, contact the Canada Revenue Agency. At age 70, you may no longer contribute.

Contributing to the post-retirement benefit does not create eligibility for, or increase the amount of, other Canada Pension Plan benefits. The post-retirement benefit cannot be divided with a former spouse (credit splitting) or shared with a current spouse (pension sharing).

For more information on the Post-Retirement Benefit, visit Canada.gc.ca.

If you work in Quebec while receiving a Canada Pension Plan or Québec Pension Plan retirement pension, for more information about contributions to the Québec Pension Plan retirement pension supplement contact Retraite Québec.

Proof of birth

You do not need to give proof of birth with your application. However, the Canada Pension Plan has the right to request proof of birth any time if they need it.

Direct deposit

Direct deposit is the fastest, most secure and most reliable way to receive your CPP payments. Nearly all CPP recipients receive their payments by direct deposit.

If your application is approved, your monthly payments will be deposited into your account at your financial institution. The account must be in your name. A joint account is also acceptable.

To enroll for direct deposit banking you must provide the branch (or transit), financial institution and account numbers that can be found in a bank book, on a bank statement or at the bottom of your cheque.

Note:

If you want to receive monthly cheques by mail, please cancel your online application. Complete the paper application for the CPP retirement pension and mail it to Service Canada.

Cheque number: If you use personalized cheques, the first set of numbers is your three-digit personal cheque number. Do not enter this number.

Branch number: The second set of numbers on personalized cheques, or the first set of numbers on non-personalized cheques, is your five-digit branch number. Enter this number in the first entry field.

Institution number: The next set of numbers is your three-digit institution number. Enter this number in the second entry field.

Account number: The last set of numbers is your bank account number. The number of digits varies depending on the financial institution. Enter your account number in the third entry field.

Tax slips

If you ask to receive your tax slips online, you may access them online each year at the beginning of February. You will not be notified when they become available.

If at any time you would prefer receiving your tax slips through the mail, you can adjust your tax slip mailing options on MSCA.

Voluntary tax deduction

Your CPP retirement pension counts as income and is taxable.

You can ask Service Canada to deduct federal income tax from your monthly pension and benefits. The deduction can be a dollar amount or a percentage of the total.

If you choose a dollar amount ($), you must use numbers with a decimal point (.) and two decimals e.g. $75.00.

A percentage (%) must not be greater than 100. Note: If you choose a percentage, it will apply to your total monthly amount, such as all of your CPP and/or OAS benefits.

Service Canada will continue to deduct federal income tax from your payments as long as you receive them, unless you ask otherwise. You can stop or change the deduction by selecting ”Federal voluntary tax deductions for CPP/OAS” in My Service Canada Account.

Benefits from other countries

If you have lived or worked in Canada and in another country, or you are the survivor of someone who has lived or worked in Canada and in another country, you may be eligible for pensions and benefits from Canada and/or from the other country. Please refer to our International Benefits page for more details.

Consent to communicate

You can name an authorized person (for example: your spouse, a member of your family, a friend or your accountant, etc.) to whom you give consent to communicate on your behalf with Service Canada about your CPP benefits.

This person that you authorize can communicate with Service Canada about your income, your CPP payments, and your CPP contributions. They can contact Service Canada to report your change of address and telephone number.

They CANNOT:

  • access My Service Canada Account (MSCA) on your behalf
  • apply for benefits for you
  • change the bank account where your payment is deposited; and
  • request or change federal income tax deductions from your benefits

This consent will remain valid until you cancel it. You can change, stop or add more authorized persons via MSCA at any time.

If Service Canada records show that a legal representative such as a trustee or someone with power of attorney is authorized to act for you, we will communicate only with that legal representative.

Child-rearing provision

If you took time off from work or worked less to look after your young children, you may be able to increase the amount of your CPP benefits by requesting the child-rearing provision.

To add children through this online service, the following conditions must be fully met:

  • you must have been the child's primary caregiver for the full period, from their birth until age of seven
  • you must have received Family Allowance or been eligible for the Canada Child Benefit (even if you did not receive it) for the full period, from their birth until age of seven
  • the child must have lived in Canada for the full period, from their birth until age of seven; and
  • you must have the child's Social Insurance Number and date of birth

If any of these conditions are not fully met, you cannot request the child-rearing provision. If you partially meet each of the conditions, you may still apply by completing the Child Rearing Provision Canada Pension Plan form and mailing it to us.

Unable to apply

If, because of a medical condition you were unable to apply earlier or to ask someone to apply on your behalf, please contact us to get a Declaration of Incapacity form. Complete and return the form to us. If you meet the eligibility requirements, you may request an earlier start date.
